Classify workloads by archetype

Four Workload Landing Zone archetypes

Archetypes describe what applications require; connectivity profiles describe what the platform provides. The mapping is not one-to-one.

1Hybrid-connected applications
Traditional line-of-business applications with tight dependencies on on-premises environments.
Typical pattern

Deploy in strategic hub regions, where the complete geographic landing zone exists.

2Isolated cloud-native or AI applications
Largely self-contained; only their application endpoints need secure exposure back into the geographic landing zones.
Typical pattern

Deploy where required services and capacity are available; expose endpoints through Private Endpoints.

3Connected cloud-native or AI applications
Need more than endpoint exposure, but do not justify recreating a complete regional hub.
Typical pattern

Private connectivity back to the geographic hubs — for example, Global VNet Peering.

4Interconnected application portfolios
A portfolio in another region needing east-west connectivity between applications and north-south connectivity to the rest of the estate.
Typical pattern

A minimally viable regional hub with only the dependencies and connectivity the portfolio needs.

Region-agnostic does not mean region-random.

Governance moves up a level

Governance is not removed; it moves into policies that do not depend on particular regions. Instead of prescribing one regional architecture for every application, define a governed operating model and a small number of approved archetypes. Each application selects the most efficient pattern that satisfies its connectivity, resiliency, data, security, and service requirements.
