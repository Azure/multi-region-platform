03 · Dimension 5 · Before workloads arrive

Platform readiness checklist for a new region

These are the platform items that most often stall a new region after it has been qualified. Most are inexpensive to plan early and expensive to retrofit. Apply only those the region’s connectivity profile and workload requirements call for.

Network foundation
IP address planningReserve non-overlapping address space for future regions and profiles. The most common blocker.
Private DNS across regionsResolution for private endpoints and hybrid names, wherever the workload runs.
Routing and inspection pathsHow traffic reaches hubs and firewalls without asymmetric paths.
Hybrid connectivityReuse existing circuits or add paths, per Dimension 5.
Governance and automation
Region settings in policyAllowed-locations and region parameters that admit the new region only for its qualified workload requirements.
Region-aware infrastructure as codeModules, naming, and tagging parameterized by region — no hard-coded locations.
Landing-zone provisioningSubscription or landing-zone vending can place workloads in the region.
Operations and data
Where logs and telemetry liveWorkspace location and residency for monitoring and security data.
Backup placementVaults and valid restore targets for the region — see Appendix A.
Runbooks and supportIncident, on-call, and support procedures include the region.
Capacity and security
Quota and specific capacityQuota for the required SKUs and models requested ahead of onboarding; capacity reservations where justified.
Key Vault placementWorkload vaults per region rather than cross-region dependency — see Appendix A.
Security coverageSecurity posture, threat detection, and privileged access paths extend to the region.

Ready for workloads means the region is qualified for its workload requirements, the required capability set is in place through standard automation, and workloads can onboard without platform exceptions.
