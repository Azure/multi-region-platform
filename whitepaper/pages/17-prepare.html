A targeted change, not a new landing zone

Prepare the region

Build only what the selected design calls for. For two of the four profiles there is no hub to build.

Preparing a region is a targeted change to the platform you already run, not a new landing zone. You build only what the selected connectivity profile and shared-service placement call for, through your existing landing-zone automation. For two of the four profiles there is no hub to build.

The Cloud Adoption Framework already describes how to add a new region to an existing landing zone, including a new hub in the region. That path is the Full Regional Hub. The other three profiles do part of it, for regions whose workloads need less.

What each profile adds

1

Disconnected Spokes

What you add in the region

The region in policy and in landing-zone provisioning. Workload spokes with their own internet ingress and egress. No hub, no gateway, no peering.

What you reuse

Governance, identity, policy, monitoring, and automation.

What to deploypage
2

Remote Hub Connected

What you add in the region

The same, plus a cross-region connection from each spoke to an existing hub, with routing and DNS settings. No hub.

What you reuse

An existing geographic hub: its gateways, firewall, DNS, and shared services.

What to deploypage
3

Minimal Regional Hub

What you add in the region

The same, plus a hub in the region with only the components you selected.

What you reuse

The geographic hub, for everything you didn’t make local.

What to deploypage
4

Full Regional Hub

What you add in the region

The same, plus a complete hub in the region.

What you reuse

The landing-zone foundation and, where they meet requirements, the existing ExpressRoute circuits.

What is global and needs no action

Most of the landing zone isn’t tied to a region:

Management groups, subscriptions, and access controlNo action. Don’t create management groups to model regions.
PolicyDefinitions and assignments apply everywhere. Update only the assignments that restrict allowed locations, so that they admit the new region.
Private DNS zonesThey are global. Create them once and link virtual networks to them.
Platform subscriptionsUse the existing connectivity and identity subscriptions, with a new resource group for the region.
