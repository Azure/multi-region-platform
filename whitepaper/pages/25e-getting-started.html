09 · Putting it into practice

Getting started

Use this sequence to establish the first additional regional options. The same process scales from one candidate region to a broader portfolio.

01

Name the drivers

Identify what is prompting the discussion — business growth, regulation, service or SKU availability, resiliency, a datacenter or connectivity event, cost, or another measurable requirement.

Drivers
02

Identify workload requirements and archetypes

Start with known workloads and application portfolios. Validate representative dependencies and identify the closest-fitting archetypes. Establish planning guardrails where future requirements are not yet known.

Dimension 3Archetypes
03

Shortlist candidate regions

Apply business and geographic requirements with mandatory data and compliance constraints. Remove regions that cannot satisfy mandatory conditions before investing in deeper technical assessment.

Dimensions 1–2
04

Qualify candidates against workload requirements

Validate services, models, SKUs, zone support, dependencies, latency, pricing, regional access, and quota. Record each option as excluded, candidate, conditional, or qualified, with evidence and revalidation triggers.

Dimension 4Qualification tree
05

Enable the most efficient platform

Select the connectivity profile, confirm the hybrid connectivity foundation, determine local versus remote shared services, and close the readiness gaps the identified workloads require.

Dimension 5Profile treeReadiness checklist
06

Onboard the first workload

Apply the placement process using current evidence. Revalidate at decision time, govern placement and resiliency decisions, and feed new requirements or platform gaps back into qualification.

Placement flow
What success looks like

Being able to qualify a regional option, enable only the platform capabilities it requires, and onboard workloads through a repeatable governed process.
