Layer 1

Resilient hybrid connectivity

Does the operating geography have resilient hybrid connectivity to Azure that satisfies the identified workload requirements?

Enabling an additional Azure region doesn’t by itself require another ExpressRoute circuit. Evaluate the existing ExpressRoute, VPN, SD-WAN, or other hybrid connectivity against datacenter and user locations, latency, bandwidth, and routing requirements. Add circuits, peering locations, gateways, or more connectivity paths only where the existing foundation can’t provide the required reachability, diversity, or resilience.

The deciding factorAre additional circuits, peering locations, gateways, or paths needed for diversity, failure-domain isolation, latency, bandwidth, routing, or resiliency?
Existing connectivity can remain sufficient — reuse the current hybrid foundation.
Change the foundation — reachability, resiliency, diversity, scale, or ownership.
Decision

Confirm the hybrid connectivity foundation and identify any required changes to reachability, resiliency, diversity, scale, or ownership.

Layers 2 – 3

Regional platform connectivity

What is the most efficient regional platform design that supports the identified scenarios?

Determine how workloads in the region will reach geographic hubs, shared platform services, application dependencies, other Azure regions, and hybrid environments. Select the profile based on those connectivity and dependency requirements:

Disconnected spokes
Remote-hub-connected spokes
Minimal regional hub
Full regional hub

Choose the profile that meets the requirements without unnecessary infrastructure. Adoption growth alone does not justify moving toward a Full Regional Hub; expand local capabilities when shared-service placement, resiliency, scale, connectivity, or operational requirements change. Profiles are described in Section 05.

Decision

Select the most efficient regional platform connectivity profile that satisfies the identified requirements and can evolve as regional adoption changes.

Hybrid ≠ Regional

Geographic hybrid connectivity and workload connectivity remain related but distinct decisions. Enabling another region does not automatically require duplicating the existing connectivity platform.
