Executive summary

Make regional choice a governed platform capability — not a one-time prediction

Many Azure estates are designed around a fixed primary and disaster-recovery region pair. This paper describes a different approach: extend Azure landing-zone principles so that additional regions become qualified options that workloads can use selectively, without creating separate governance models or reproducing an identical platform footprint in every region.

The objective is not to prebuild every capability everywhere. It is to establish a consistent operating model, qualify regional options against known business and workload requirements, and enable only the platform capabilities that each region needs.

Extend, do not replicate

The landing-zone operating model stays consistent across the Azure estate. New regions add only the most efficient platform capabilities required by the workloads expected to use them, rather than replicating an existing regional footprint.

Qualify regions against defined requirements

Regional qualification evaluates business and geographic needs, data and compliance constraints, workload requirements and dependencies, regional capabilities, and platform enablement considerations. A region can remain excluded, conditional, or qualified based on documented evidence, constraints, owners, and revalidation triggers.

Qualification creates a governed regional option; it does not permanently approve the region for every workload.

Plan by workload requirements, not individual applications

Workload Landing Zone archetypes provide a planning abstraction for recurring connectivity and dependency requirements. They help platform teams anticipate what future workloads may require without attempting to predict every application in advance.

Archetypes describe what applications require. Regional connectivity profiles describe what the platform provides. The relationship is intentionally not one-to-one.

Executive summary · continued

Right-size regional connectivity

Regional Platform Connectivity Profiles range from Disconnected Spokes through Remote Hub Connected and Minimal Regional Hub to a Full Regional Hub.

These profiles are not maturity stages. Select the most efficient profile for current workload and platform requirements. Add local capabilities only when a measurable need for connectivity, latency, resiliency, security, data handling, or operations justifies them.

Strategic geographic hubs and shared services can continue to support other regions where dependencies and service characteristics permit, avoiding unnecessary duplication.

Keep platform readiness and workload placement separate

Regional qualification determines whether a region can become a viable platform option. Actual workload placement occurs later, when a specific workload and its current requirements are known.

During onboarding, validate current Azure service and SKU availability, regional access, quota, dependencies, connectivity, and resiliency requirements. Placement, relocation, recovery, and multi-region operation remain workload-specific decisions.

New workload findings can also feed back into regional qualification and trigger platform expansion, additional validation, or reassessment.

Keep the operating model consistent while allowing regional adaptation

The operating model stays consistent across regions; implementation can vary where business, workload, or regulatory requirements call for it.

Workload placement, connectivity profiles, local shared-service footprints, Azure services and SKUs, resiliency choices, and other regional parameters can adapt to current requirements.

Region-agnostic does not mean region-random. Regional choice operates within a governed framework of qualified options, documented constraints, and current evidence.

The goal

The goal is not to select the perfect two regions. It is to make additional regions significantly easier to adopt when business and workload requirements justify them.
